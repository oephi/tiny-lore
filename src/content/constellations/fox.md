The fox woke to find the sky empty. No moon. Not even a sliver.

"It must have fallen," she said, and set off through the midnight forest to find it.

She checked the bottom of the old well. She checked inside the hollow oak. She even checked under Mrs. Badger's porch, which took some courage because Mrs. Badger did not appreciate visitors.

At last she came to the river, and there it was — the moon, sitting in the water, round and bright as a silver coin.

The fox reached in to grab it, but her paw went right through. She tried again. And again. Each time the moon broke into a hundred shimmering pieces, then slowly put itself back together.

She sat back on her haunches, thinking. Then she looked up — and there was the moon, right where it had always been, hanging in the sky above the trees.

"Oh," said the fox. She looked back at the river. The moon was there too. She looked up again. Two moons. "Well," she said, "that's twice as nice." And she trotted home, quite pleased with the world.
