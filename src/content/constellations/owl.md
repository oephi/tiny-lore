The owl had read every book in the forest. This was not as impressive as it sounds, because there was only one book, and it was written by a beetle who had very strong opinions about bark.

Still, the owl considered herself well-read, and she took her job as the forest librarian very seriously.

Every night she would perch on the highest branch and tell stories to whoever would listen. The trouble was, she only knew the one story — the beetle's book — and it wasn't very exciting.

"Chapter Forty-Seven," she would begin. "In which I discover a particularly satisfying piece of elm bark." The audience would shuffle. Leaves would be examined. Yawns would spread like ripples.

Then one evening, a small rabbit asked, "What if you just... made something up?"

The owl blinked her enormous eyes. Made something up? Was that allowed? She opened her beak, and out came a story about a castle made of moonlight, and a key made of song, and a door that only opened when you laughed.

The forest leaned in. Even the wind stopped to listen. And the owl, for the first time in her life, forgot all about bark.
