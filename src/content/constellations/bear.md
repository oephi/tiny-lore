Deep in the oldest part of the forest, where the trees grew so tall their tops disappeared into clouds, there lived a bear with fur the colour of autumn leaves.

She was not the biggest bear, nor the strongest. But she was the most patient. Every evening she would sit at the edge of the great clearing and watch the sky turn from gold to violet to deep, deep blue.

The smaller animals would come to sit beside her — rabbits, hedgehogs, even the shy little mice — because they knew that nothing bad could happen when the bear was watching.

"What are you looking for?" a tiny mouse once asked her.

The bear smiled. "I'm not looking for anything," she said. "I'm making sure everything is still here."

And so each night she counted the stars, one by one, until she was certain that not a single one had gone missing. Only then would she close her eyes and sleep, knowing the forest was safe for another night.
