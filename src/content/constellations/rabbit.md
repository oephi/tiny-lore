Everyone knew that rabbits were good at hopping. But this particular rabbit was good at hopping between places that weren't quite there.

One hop and she'd be in a meadow made entirely of music, where the grass played different notes when the wind blew through it. Another hop and she'd find herself in a library where the books read themselves aloud in sleepy, rumbling voices.

She tried to tell the other rabbits about these places, but they were mostly interested in clover.

"There's a whole world made of rain," she'd say. "Every drop is a tiny bell."

"That's nice," they'd reply. "Have you tried the clover by the south fence? It's exceptional this year."

So the rabbit kept her worlds to herself, visiting them one by one each night after the others had gone to sleep. She'd hop through doorways only she could see, into places only she could find, and return each morning with grass stains on her knees and stars caught in her fur.
