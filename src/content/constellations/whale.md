In the deepest part of the ocean, where the water was so dark it was almost a colour no one had invented yet, there lived a whale who sang.

Her songs were not like other songs. They didn't have beginnings or endings. They were more like questions that didn't need answers, or like the feeling you get when you remember something beautiful that might not have happened.

The other sea creatures would stop what they were doing when she sang. Jellyfish would pause mid-pulse. Crabs would forget which direction they were walking. Even the grumpy old octopus would uncross at least three of his arms.

One day a small fish asked her, "What are your songs about?"

The whale thought for a long time. Whales are good at thinking for a long time.

"They're about the space between things," she said at last. "Between the surface and the deep. Between sleeping and waking. Between one wave and the next."

The small fish didn't entirely understand, but it didn't matter. Understanding wasn't really the point. The point was the listening, and the way the dark water hummed, and the feeling that even in the deepest, darkest place, you were never quite alone.
